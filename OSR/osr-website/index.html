Student Help

What should I do if I need help or want to bring something to the Student Regent?

Three ways in: raise a concern as a student, rate an OSR service, or file an official document on behalf of your council or organization. The concern form saves as you type.

Raise a concern

Step 1 of 3

Be factual: what happened, where and when, who is affected, what resolution you seek.

Privacy notice

What we collect
Your name, student number, and email. Choose Submit anonymously and we collect none of them — only an email address if you want a reply.
Why we collect it
So the OSR can confirm what happened, act on your concern, and answer you. Your student number shows the concern comes from a BulSU student.
Who can see it
OSR staff only. Your details are never published on this site and never shown to other students.
How long we keep it
Kept while your concern is being handled, and afterwards as part of the OSR record of it.

After you submit you get a tracking code. Use it on Track My Concern to see when the OSR responds.

Rate the BulSU OSR

Rate an OSR service. Your name and student number are required so each rating comes from a real student.

Privacy notice

What we collect
Your name, student number, campus, the service you rate, your rating and comment, and an email address only if you want a reply.
Why we collect it
So every rating comes from an identified BulSU student and the office can follow up on what you report.
Who can see it
OSR staff only. Your name and student number are never published; the public Pulse and reports carry totals, not individual ratings.
How long we keep it
Kept while your concern is being handled, and afterwards as part of the OSR record of it.

Organization & council submissions

Not for individuals

For student councils, college councils, and recognized student organizations. Send a position, resolution, request, or other official statement — signed by your organization — for the OSR to receive, consider, and publish. Proposals endorsed to the Student Policy Development Office (SPDO) are filed here too.

Filing as an individual student? Use Raise a concern above instead.

Privacy notice

What we collect
Your organization's name, campus, the type of submission, its title, your contact email, and the signed document you upload.
Why we collect it
So the OSR can verify the document comes from your organization, record it, and reply to the person who filed it.
Who can see it
OSR staff review every submission. The organization's name and the document may be published on this site once the office accepts the filing — your contact email is never published.
How long we keep it
Kept while your concern is being handled, and afterwards as part of the OSR record of it.

  2. Brings student concerns, positions and proposals into university governance

    How OSR uses the Pulse

    Listen

    Build priorities provide an additional way for the OSR to hear what students are currently emphasizing.

    Consult

    Results can help inform consultations and identify areas that may need further engagement.

    Discuss

    Recurring or notable priorities may help inform possible programs or matters prepared for Board Meetings.

    Follow up

    Where appropriate, the OSR can communicate developments or next steps connected to issues raised.

    Not every result becomes a program, reaches the Board, or guarantees action — participation is voluntary consultation.

    About the Pulse expand

    Each participant receives 10 points and decides how to distribute them across the 7 areas. Submissions are anonymous and aggregated. Results are a student pulse — voluntary consultation, not a binding vote, election, or scientifically representative survey, and should not be assumed to represent every BulSU student.

    Periods and percentages depend only on actual stored responses. The dashboard never downloads raw responses — only small aggregate objects per period are loaded and percentages are calculated locally.

    These results provide the OSR an additional way to listen

    Recurring student priorities are one source of input when the OSR considers consultations, possible initiatives, and matters for discussion. Language: student pulse, student priorities, anonymous student input, consultation data, collective responses.
